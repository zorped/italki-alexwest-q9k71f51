Phrases

Say what you mean

You like to explain your thinking. These four openers let you start a sentence in Hindi, even when you are not sure how it ends.

  • Main yeh kehna chahta hoon ki…

    I want to say that…

  • Mera matlab hai ki…

    I mean that…

  • Mujhe lagta hai ki…

    I think that…

  • Isko Hindi mein kya bolte hain?

    What do you call this in Hindi?

Learn them as one pieceDo not build these word by word. Learn Main yeh kehna chahta hoon ki… as a single chunk, then finish it with your own idea.

2

Sentences

Talk about your work

Built from what you told me about your acting and history. Change anything that is not true for you.

  • Main films mein kaam karta hoon.

    I work in films.

  • Maine saat-aath Hindi films mein kaam kiya hai.

    I have worked in seven or eight Hindi films.

  • Maine pehli baar 2017 mein Hindi film mein kaam kiya tha.

    I first worked in a Hindi film in 2017.

  • Maine ek angrez police wale ka role kiya tha.

    I played an English police officer.

  • Jab main koi character karta hoon, toh mujhe samajhna padta hai ki woh kaise sochta hai.

    When I play a character, I have to understand how he thinks.

  • Mujhe jaanne ka bahut mann karta hai ki doosre log kaise sochte hain.

    I really want to know how other people think.

  • Mere liye acting sirf kaam nahin hai, mujhe isme bahut maza aata hai.

    For me, acting is not just work. I enjoy it a lot.

  • Mujhe India aur England ki history aur dono ke rishton mein bahut interest hai.

    I am very interested in the history of India and England, and the relationship between them.

English words are finePeople in Delhi mix in English words all day: film, role, character, history, interest. You can too. It sounds natural.

Kiya hai or kiya tha?Kiya hai means something you have done, and it still counts now. Kiya tha means it happened and finished, like 2017.

Words that sound alike

You already know ghoos, a bribe. Here it sits with its look-alikes, plus two more pairs from your own sentences. A small sound difference changes the word completely.

Ghus · ghoos · ghoom

short u · long oo · oo with m

  • Ghus jaana, get inside: Main apne character mein ghus jaata hoon.
  • Ghoos, a bribe: Usne officer ko ghoos di.
  • Ghoomna, wander around: Hum Sunday ko Delhi mein ghoomne gaye the.

Kaam or kam

long aa · short a

  • Kaam, work: Aaj mere paas bahut kaam hai.
  • Kam, less: Aaj traffic kam hai.
  • Both together: Aaj kaam kam hai. There is little work today.

Padhna or padna

with breath on dh · without

  • Padhna, read or study: Maine uske baare mein bahut padha hai.
  • Padna, have to: Mujhe samajhna padta hai.

Postpositions and fast speakers

You told me postpositions and fast speech are hard. Hindi postpositions work like English prepositions, but they come after the noun: Delhi mein, not in Delhi. Here are the six you will use most, and four phrases for when someone speaks too fast.

Six postpositions

they come after the noun

  • mein = in: Main films mein kaam karta hoon.
  • par = on, at: Meri book table par hai.
  • se = from, by: Main England se hoon.
  • ko = to someone: Maine usko bata diya.
  • ke saath = with: Main apne dost ke saath gaya.
  • ke liye = for: Yeh gift tumhare liye hai.

When someone speaks too fast

say one of these, calmly

  • Thoda dheere boliye. Please speak a little slower.
  • Ek baar phir se boliye. Please say it once more.
  • Main samjha nahin. I did not understand.
  • Iska matlab kya hai? What does that mean?

Check yourself

  1. Main England     hoon.

  2. Main films     kaam karta hoon.

  3. Meri book table     hai.

  4. Main apne dost ke     gaya.

  5. Yeh gift tumhare     hai.

Fast speech and accentsUnderstanding fast speakers and different accents comes from short, repeated listening, not from grammar. We will work on it together in class, one short clip at a time.
